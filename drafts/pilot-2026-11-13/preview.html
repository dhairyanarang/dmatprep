A fund's banded annual charge

p1 A savings fund takes one charge a year. It is worked out in bands on the balance the member held at the start of the year, and each band's rate applies only to the part of the balance that falls inside that band — not to the whole balance. The bands are read from the bottom upwards.

t1 The bands, and the rate that applies to the part of the opening balance falling in each.
Part of the opening balanceRate on that part
The first 20,000 euros1.20%
The next 30,000 euros0.80%
Anything above 50,000 euros0.40%

p2 Whatever the bands produce, the annual charge is never more than 320 euros. Where the banded total comes to more than that, 320 euros is taken instead and the excess is waived.

p3 The net return is the gross return for the year less the annual charge, both in euros, then stated as a percentage of the opening balance. The gross return is given before any charge is taken, and no other deduction applies.

A member's balance at the start of the year is 80,000 euros, and the fund's gross return for the year is 4,800 euros. What is the member's net return?

  • (a) 4.80%
  • (b) 5.25%
  • (c) 5.60% ✓
  • (d) 6.00%

The bands are applied to parts of the balance, not to the whole of it: 20,000 at 1.20% is 240 euros, the next 30,000 at 0.80% is another 240, and the 30,000 that sits above 50,000 at 0.40% is 120 — 600 euros in total. But p2 caps the charge at 320 euros, so 320 is what is actually taken. The net return is therefore 4,800 − 320 = 4,480 euros, and p3 says to state that as a percentage of the opening balance: 4,480 ÷ 80,000 = 5.60%.
