# Copyright and exam-confidentiality review

Conducted 14 November 2026 over all active Core and GAM content.

**This is a technical review, not a legal opinion.** Nothing here clears the
product for release, and one finding below is explicitly referred out.

## The standard we are measured against

Quoted verbatim from the official Terms and Conditions at
`d-mat.de/en/terms-conditions/`, read 14 November 2026:

> **§9.1** "All working documents issued or information provided to the
> participant are protected by copyright. Without written permission from
> g.a.s.t., no participant or third party may reproduce these documents or
> information in any form, **including for the purposes of teaching**, and in
> particular process, duplicate, distribute, use for public reproduction, pass
> on to third parties or make them available to the public using electronic
> systems."

> **§9.1** "The disclosure, publication and making available to third parties of
> any test items and test materials issued in the examination, as well as their
> posting on social networks, internet forums, websites and similar media is
> prohibited."

> **§6.15** "You are not allowed to record any information about the content of
> the dMAT and take it with you after the examination."

> **§6.16** "You are not allowed to discuss content of the exam with other test
> takers, to pass it on to third parties or to make it available to the public."

## The one finding that needs a lawyer

**§9.1 prohibits reproduction of official material "in any form, including for
the purposes of teaching", and we reproduce four short attributed passages from
the preparatory materials in the section guides.**

What we do: four quotations, the longest about 45 words, each inside an explicit
quote block with a page citation, used to state what the official material
itself says about a task type before the guide explains it. The rest of every
guide is paraphrase.

Why it is not obviously cleared by the attribution: §9.1 names teaching
explicitly as a purpose that is *not* exempted, and it requires written
permission rather than attribution. Whether short quotation for criticism,
review or reporting falls within fair dealing notwithstanding a contractual
prohibition is a question of law, in a specific jurisdiction, about a contract
whose counterparty is in Germany. It is not a question a technical review can
answer, and I am not going to answer it.

**Three options, for a lawyer to choose between, not for me:**

1. **Ask g.a.s.t. for written permission** for the four passages. Cleanest, and
   the one §9.1 actually describes.
2. **Replace all four with paraphrase.** Costs some precision — the value of the
   quote is that it is the official wording — but removes the question. This is
   the only option implementable without anyone's permission.
3. **Rely on fair dealing** with the quotations and attributions as they stand.
   Defensible-sounding and not something to rely on without advice.

Note that §9.1 binds "any participant or third party", so being independent of
g.a.s.t. does not place us outside it.
